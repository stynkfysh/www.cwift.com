Homes in East San Jose / Mayfair

Mayfair, east of downtown, is one of San Jose's older flatland neighborhoods, with small 1940s and 1950s houses and cottages on narrow lots, some with rear additions or second structures. Farther east, Alum Rock and the streets off Story Road, King Road and White Road hold postwar ranch tracts from the 1950s and 1960s. The East Foothills, partly unincorporated county land, climb toward Alum Rock Park with larger hillside lots and valley views. Condos and townhomes sit along Capitol Expressway and the main boulevards, with Mount Hamilton rising behind.

What moves home values in East San Jose / Mayfair

Lot depth and rear structures vary widely in Mayfair; a deep lot with room for an ADU is compared with similar lots, and existing back units are described from photos and records.

County pockets in the East Foothills and Alum Rock follow Santa Clara County permits, not city rules, so permit history is checked with the right agency before additions are credited.

Hillside lots below Alum Rock Park have views and space but steeper driveways and some wildfire exposure; lot photos and slope are weighed against similar foothill sales.

Homes near Reid-Hillview Airport, Capitol Expressway or Highway 101 can sell differently from those on quiet streets; comparables are drawn from homes with similar surroundings.

Each of these is documented from your photos, permits and records and reflected in the comparable-sales adjustments.

Questions from East San Jose / Mayfair

My parents converted their East San Jose garage without permits; does it count?

Not as regular living area. Unpermitted space is reported separately and valued for the utility buyers give it, which is usually less than the same square footage inside the original house. The loss of covered parking is weighed too. Photos of the converted room and the exterior help, and if permits were later finaled with the city or county, share the record so it can be credited.
