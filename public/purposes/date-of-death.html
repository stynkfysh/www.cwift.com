24-Hour Date-of-Death Appraisals for the Step-Up in Basis

Retrospective value on the date of death to support the heirs' new tax basis.

In plain English

When someone dies and leaves a house, this appraisal says what the house was worth on the day they died. The heirs and their tax preparer use that number as the starting point for any tax when the house is sold.

When a California homeowner dies, the heirs' tax basis in the house generally resets to what the home was worth on the day of death. That number decides how much capital gain an heir reports when the property sells, and escrow, a tax preparer's calendar or an eager buyer can leave very little time to produce it. Why estates end up needing the number fast

Many families learn they need a basis appraisal late in the process: the house is already in escrow, the CPA is finishing the final returns, or a trustee is about to distribute proceeds. A 24-hour report keeps the sale and the filing moving without falling back on an online estimate that won't hold up.

What the appraisal documents

  • The home's market value on the day of death, or on the alternate valuation date six months later when the executor elects it on the estate tax return under IRC §2032.
  • The real estate figure on a federal estate tax return (Form 706), whether the estate is large enough to owe tax or a surviving spouse files only to keep the unused exemption (portability). California has no estate tax of its own.
  • Comparable sales that closed around the valuation date rather than today's market.
  • The home's condition as it was then, reconstructed from your photos and records when it has changed since.
  • Support for California's community property rule, under which both halves of community property can receive a new basis when one spouse dies (IRC §1014(b)(6)).

What to send

  • The date of death and the property address
  • Photos that show the home as it was at the date of death; older snapshots and prior listing photos are welcome
  • A short note on anything that has changed since: repairs, clean-out, remodeling, damage
  • Who will rely on the report, such as a CPA, an attorney or a trustee

Send photos with your order through the upload page, or attach them to a reply to your confirmation email.

Is a desktop appraisal right for this?

Date-of-death work is where desktop appraisals make the most sense. Nobody can walk through the house as it stood on a past date; the analysis rests on sales from that period and on evidence of the home's condition at the time. A site visit today would document today's house, which may not be the house that matters.

If beneficiaries are likely to argue about the value, an inspection-based appraisal can carry more weight. Ask your attorney before ordering.

Questions

How do I find out what my mom's house was worth when she died?

Order a date-of-death appraisal. Give the date of death and send any photos from around that time, even old snapshots or listing photos. The appraiser studies sales from that period and reports the home's market value as of that day, which is the figure your tax preparer needs.

Does the estate have to file Form 706, the federal estate tax return?

Only estates above the federal basic exclusion amount ($15 million per person for deaths in 2026) owe estate tax, but some smaller estates file anyway: a surviving spouse keeps the late spouse's unused exemption only by filing Form 706. The return is due nine months after death, with a six-month extension available, and Rev. Proc. 2022-32 allows a late portability-only return up to five years after death for estates not otherwise required to file. Either way, the date-of-death appraisal supports the real estate value. Your CPA or estate attorney decides whether to file.

Can you appraise a date of death from many years ago?

Yes. Retrospective appraisals are routine, and Brian has completed them for dates several decades back. Very old dates can require extra research, so 24-hour delivery is confirmed before you pay.

Will the IRS accept the report?

The IRS doesn't pre-approve appraisers or report formats. It looks for a credible, well-supported valuation by a qualified appraiser. Cwift reports are prepared under USPAP and signed by a California Certified Residential appraiser, and your tax advisor can confirm what your return needs.

The house was remodeled after the death. Is that a problem?

No. Send whatever shows the earlier condition: old listing photos, family pictures, permits or contractor invoices. The value is developed as of the date of death, not as the house looks now.
