The 24-hour service

When does the 24-hour clock start?

When both your payment and your photos and property details have arrived. From that moment the appraisal is emailed to you within 24 hours, and the clock runs seven days a week, weekends and holidays included.

What happens if I finish my order on a Saturday night?

Nothing changes. Weekends and holidays count, so if payment and photos are in at 9 p.m. Saturday, the report is in your inbox by 9 p.m. Sunday.

Is 24-hour delivery available for every property?

For most California single-family homes and condominiums, yes. Very old valuation dates, highly unusual properties and orders covering several properties can need more time. Every request is reviewed before invoicing, so you'll know before you pay.

Why does the 24-hour service cost more?

The 24-hour fee is the standard fee plus 50%, rounded to the nearest $5: $450 instead of $299 for a Basic Desktop and $675 instead of $449 for a Standard Desktop. It pays for putting your file at the front of the line, every day of the week.

What if I don't actually need it within 24 hours?

Order the same report at the standard fee ($299 or $449) with delivery in 5–10 business days. The analysis is identical; only the timing changes.

Desktop appraisals

What is a desktop appraisal?

A complete appraisal prepared without a site visit by the appraiser. The appraiser researches the market, selects and adjusts comparable sales, and reconciles a value exactly as in any appraisal, describing the home from your photos, public records, listing history and the details you supply.

Is a desktop appraisal the same as a Zestimate or a broker price opinion?

No. Automated estimates are computer models with no appraiser and no accountability, and broker opinions come from licensees who aren't appraisers. A desktop appraisal is developed and signed by a state-certified appraiser under USPAP, with a certification, a workfile and a license that can be checked.

Are desktop appraisals USPAP-compliant?

Yes. USPAP leaves the extent of any inspection to the appraiser's scope of work and requires the report to state whether the appraiser personally inspected the property. Cwift reports state plainly that they are desktop appraisals and describe the information relied on.

When is a desktop appraisal the wrong choice?

Mortgage lending, cases headed to trial where the other side will challenge every assumption, disputes among beneficiaries, courts or agencies that require an interior inspection, and properties whose condition or features photos can't capture. For those, Brian Ward Appraisal offers inspection-based appraisals.

Have you done desktop appraisals for my purpose before?

Brian Ward has prepared desktop appraisals for every purpose listed on this site. Some situations still call for an inspection-based appraisal, so check with your attorney or tax advisor before you order to be sure a desktop report fits your matter.

Ordering, photos and delivery

Why is there no phone number?

Because email and the online form are faster. A written request arrives with the address, the dates and the purpose already spelled out, photos travel in the same message, and nothing waits on a callback. Send questions to contact@cwift.com at any hour.

What photos do you need?

The front and back of the house, the kitchen, each bathroom, the main living areas and bedrooms, plus anything that adds or subtracts value: upgrades, a pool, a view, damage, unfinished work. Smartphone photos are fine. For a past date, send photos that show the home as it was then.

Can I send photos before I pay?

Yes, and it saves time. Upload them right after you submit your order, or reply to the confirmation email with them. The clock starts the moment payment arrives.

How is the appraisal delivered?

As a PDF emailed to you, ready to forward to your CPA, attorney, trustee or the agency that asked for it.

What if the report gets something wrong about my property?

If a feature, the condition or the quality of the home was misread or misdescribed, the appraisal is revised at no charge. Tell us what's different and include anything that shows it.

What kinds of property do you appraise?

California single-family homes and condominiums, including townhomes and PUD homes. Cwift does not appraise 2–4 unit buildings, apartment buildings, commercial property and vacant land. Luxury homes, large acreage and highly unique properties may need a custom quote.

Do you cover my county?

Yes. Desktop appraisals are available for property in all 58 California counties.

Can I use the appraisal to get a mortgage or refinance?

No. Lenders must order their own appraisals, and an appraisal can't be readdressed to a new client later. Cwift reports are for tax, legal, estate and personal decisions.

Who actually prepares the appraisal?

Brian Ward, California Certified Residential Real Estate Appraiser, license AR036053, who has appraised California homes since 2004. No trainees, no outsourcing.

How do I pay?

Through the secure online invoice emailed to you after your request is reviewed. Payment and photos start the 24-hour clock.
