24-Hour Appraisals for Prop 19 Parent-Child Transfers

Market value on the transfer date, to see how much of the old tax base survives.

In plain English

When parents pass their home to a child, Prop 19 decides how much of the old, low property tax stays. The home's market value on the transfer date is the number that decides it.

Since February 16, 2021, Proposition 19 has narrowed California's parent-child property tax exclusion. A family home keeps part of its low assessment only when a child, or a qualifying grandchild, makes it their principal residence, and only up to a value cap. The home's market value on the transfer date tells you how much of the old assessment survives.

The numbers drive several decisions at once

Move in, rent it out, sell it, or buy out siblings: each choice changes the tax bill, and families often have to decide quickly. The assessor's claim forms carry their own deadlines too.

How the Prop 19 cap works

  • For transfers from February 16, 2025 through February 15, 2027, the exclusion covers the parent's factored base year value plus $1,044,586. Market value above that limit is added to the assessment (State Board of Equalization).
  • Earlier windows used $1,000,000 (Feb 16, 2021 to Feb 15, 2023) and $1,022,600 (through Feb 15, 2025); the amount adjusts every two years.
  • Parent-to-child transfers are claimed on form BOE-19-P with the county assessor; grandparent-to-grandchild transfers use BOE-19-G.

What to send

  • The transfer date, which is often the date of death
  • Photos and notes on the home's condition
  • Which way the transfer runs: parent to child, child to parent, or grandparent to grandchild

Is a desktop appraisal right for this?

County assessors set values from sales data and records, generally without walking through the house. A desktop appraisal works from the same kind of evidence, examined property by property, which makes it a natural fit for Prop 19 planning and for questioning an assessor's figure.

Questions

Will my property taxes go up if I inherit my parents' house?

Often, yes. If you don't make it your main home, the county reassesses it at market value. If you move in and claim the exclusion, part of the old assessment can carry over, up to a cap. An appraisal of the home's value on the transfer date tells you which way it goes.

Does the appraisal set my new property tax?

No. The county assessor sets the assessed value. The appraisal gives you an independent market value to plan with and, if needed, evidence for a review or appeal of the assessor's figure.

What if the assessor's market value seems too high?

An independent appraisal of market value on the transfer date is the evidence you would bring to an informal review or a formal assessment appeal.
