24-Hour Appraisals for Property Bonds and Bail Collateral

Current value to document equity for a property bond or bail collateral.

In plain English

To use a house instead of cash for bail, the court needs proof that the owner's equity is at least twice the bail. A current appraisal is that proof.

California lets a property owner post real estate equity instead of cash bail under Penal Code §1298, but the court must find that the equity is at least twice the bail amount. That finding starts with a current appraisal.

What courts and bail agents look for

  • Superior courts publish their own property-bond instructions, and many call for an appraisal by a California-licensed or certified appraiser with the license number on the report.
  • Some courts require the appraisal to be recent, for example within the last six months, and some specify a report form.
  • Bail agents who accept real estate as collateral often want an appraisal to confirm the equity.

What to send

  • The court and case number, or the bail agent's name
  • The bail amount and any required report format
  • Photos, plus your mortgage balance(s) for your own equity calculation

Questions

Can I use my house for bail in California?

Often, yes. Under Penal Code §1298 a court can accept equity in real estate instead of cash if the equity is at least twice the bail amount. Each court sets its own paperwork, and a current appraisal is usually part of it.

Does the equity need to be twice the bail?

Under Penal Code §1298 the court must find that the equity is at least twice the bail amount. Equity is the appraised value minus the loans and liens against the property.

Do bail agents accept property as collateral?

Many do. Ask the agent which report and effective date they need before you order.
