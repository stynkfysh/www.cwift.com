Why desktop appraisals work for most purposes

A desktop appraisal is a complete appraisal. The only thing it leaves out is the appraiser's site visit, and for most non-lending purposes that visit adds less than people expect.

What a desktop appraisal is

A desktop appraisal is developed and reported by a licensed or certified appraiser under the Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice (USPAP), the standards that govern appraisals throughout the United States. The appraiser identifies the property, researches the market, selects comparable sales, adjusts them for their differences, reconciles a value and signs a certification. Instead of walking through the home, the appraiser describes it from photographs, public records, prior listings and information from the people who know it.

People also call a desktop appraisal a virtual appraisal, a remote appraisal, an online home appraisal or an appraisal without an inspection. Whatever the name, it is a full appraisal by a state-certified appraiser, made without a site visit.

It is not an automated valuation model, an online estimate or a broker price opinion. Those are estimates. A desktop appraisal is an appraisal, with a named, accountable appraiser behind it.

Five reasons it holds up

1. The value comes from the analysis

Ask a reviewer what makes an appraisal credible and you'll hear about the comparables and the reasoning, not the walk-through. Both are identical in a desktop report.

2. Comparable sales are never inspected

Even in a traditional appraisal, the comparables are studied remotely through MLS records, photos and public data. A desktop applies the same method to the subject home.

3. Owners know their homes

A walk-through lasts about half an hour. Owners, heirs and trustees know about the new roof, the unpermitted patio and the bathroom nobody has touched in decades, and their photos can show it.

4. Past dates can't be inspected

Most purposes on this site involve a past date. A visit today shows today's house; the analysis has to reconstruct the house as it was.

5. The mortgage market already uses them

Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac accept desktop appraisals for certain purchase loans, where the house secures hundreds of thousands of borrowed dollars.

When to choose an inspection-based appraisal instead

  • Any mortgage, refinance or other lending decision; lenders must order their own appraisals.
  • A case headed to trial where the other side will challenge every assumption.
  • Beneficiaries or co-owners who are already disputing the value.
  • A court, agency or agreement that specifically requires an interior inspection.
  • A property with major unpermitted additions, significant damage, or features photos can't capture.
